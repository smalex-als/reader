Take the chapter text I give you and rewrite it into a sharper, more useful system design note.

Goals:
- remove trivial, repetitive, or interview-theater content
- keep only the parts that are specific, non-obvious, and architecturally important
- make the result more useful for actual understanding, not just for preserving the original chapter structure

What to remove or compress:
- repetitive interviewer / candidate dialogue
- obvious statements that do not add design insight
- generic API boilerplate unless it matters to the design
- long introductions and filler wrap-up sections
- broad textbook explanations of common technologies unless they are directly relevant
- generic system design advice that could apply to any problem
- low-value implementation trivia that does not change architectural decisions

What to keep and strengthen:
- the real problem framing
- the most important requirements and constraints
- scale assumptions that actually affect the design
- the core design choice and why it fits
- tradeoffs between plausible approaches
- correctness concerns
- failure modes and recovery
- operational and product-facing consequences of bad design choices
- the first real scaling boundary, where the solution stops being simple

Writing style:
- write in clear, direct English
- optimize for signal, not completeness
- prefer explanation over transcription
- do not preserve the original chapter’s verbosity
- do not pad the text
- if something is obvious, compress it
- if something is subtle or important, expand it

Output structure:
- produce a polished standalone write-up
- use clear section headings
- keep a logical flow from problem -> requirements -> scale -> classification -> design -> correctness -> recovery -> scaling limits -> final recommendation

Important:
- do not just summarize the chapter
- improve it
- if the original text spends too much space on low-value material, replace that space with deeper explanation of the parts that actually matter
- make the result feel like the chapter was rewritten by someone with stronger engineering judgment
