Take the multi-page system design interview chapter text I give you and compress it into a high-signal review extract that can be reread quickly before an interview.

Goal:
- produce a compact 1 to 2 page extract
- optimize for recall, not completeness
- keep only the ideas that materially help someone remember the design and explain it under pressure

What this extract is for:
- a fast refresher before an interview
- a memory aid after the full chapter has already been read
- a compact review document, not a full rewrite and not a tiny memory card
- a way to reconstruct the design discussion from memory without rereading the full chapter

Core rules:
- write in clear direct English
- keep the strongest technical signal
- remove filler, repeated dialogue, and interview theater
- compress obvious material aggressively
- expand only the parts that matter for design reasoning
- do not preserve the chapter’s original pacing if it wastes space
- preserve concrete numbers, constraints, bottlenecks, and architectural pivots when they affect the design
- if the chapter contains multiple candidate designs, explain why the final one wins

What to include:
- the real problem framing
- the few requirements and constraints that actually drive the design
- the scale assumptions that change architecture choices
- the core design and why it fits
- the write path and read path if they matter
- the critical data model or state model
- tradeoffs between the main candidate approaches
- correctness risks, failure modes, and recovery points
- the first important scaling boundary or limitation
- interviewer-ready talking points: what to say, what to avoid, and what decision demonstrates senior judgment
- the final recommended design intuition

What to exclude or compress heavily:
- repetitive interviewer / candidate dialogue
- broad textbook explanations of common components
- generic advice that applies to any system design problem
- low-value API boilerplate
- restatements that do not add new insight
- long intros and long conclusions
- implementation trivia that does not change the architecture
- definitions of standard components unless the chapter uses them in a non-obvious way

Writing style:
- sound like a sharp senior engineer preparing notes for review
- prefer dense but readable explanation
- use short sections with descriptive headings
- use bullets where they improve scanability
- keep paragraphs compact
- avoid fluff and motivational language
- be specific: name the component, responsibility, data shape, constraint, or tradeoff instead of saying "use a database" or "scale the system"

Output structure:
- start with a short section: "Core idea"
- then use concise sections such as:
  - Problem and constraints
  - Scale that matters
  - Chosen design
  - Data model / state model
  - Critical flows
  - Tradeoffs
  - Failure modes and correctness
  - Limits and evolution
  - Interview takeaway
- merge or omit sections when they are not useful
- do not include every listed section mechanically; optimize for the chapter's actual content

Length target:
- aim for roughly 700 to 1200 words
- short enough to reread quickly
- long enough to preserve the important architecture and tradeoffs
- if the chapter is very dense, prefer the upper end of the range; if it is repetitive, prefer the lower end

Important:
- do not produce a generic summary
- do not turn it into flashcards
- do not make it conversational
- do not add information not supported by the source
- make the output feel like an interview review sheet derived from the chapter
- if the source is ambiguous, keep the ambiguity visible instead of inventing missing details

Output requirements:
- output only the final extract
- no markdown fences
- no meta commentary about what you did
